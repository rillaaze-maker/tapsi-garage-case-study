Insight · Jobs-to-be-Done

Same product, two different jobs.

I gave both situations equal priority.

Emergency

Stuck on the road

When my car won't start and I'm stuck on the road, I want someone to come and get it running, so I can get back on my way without knowing anything about batteries.

NeedsHelp where the car is · One decision · How soon

Planned

Replacing it before it dies

When my battery is getting weak, I want the right battery for my car at a fair price, installed when it suits me, so I don't end up stranded later.

NeedsCertainty it fits · Honest comparison · My time

Problem

What, why, how.

What
Two ways to get a battery. The catalog sells it like engine oil and sends you to a shop. The on-site service is hidden, runs on a partner's web page, and books a time window.
WhyThe core insight
Both were built as purchases, product first. For a battery, the situation comes first: a dead car needs help fast, a planned swap needs certainty it fits.
How
One entry that asks what's going on. An emergency path built for speed. A planned path that starts from your car and joins shop and at-home installation.

Solution · References

A stranded driver already knows this flow.

I took a Snapp ride and screenshotted every step. Choose a type, request, wait, watch it arrive. My emergency screen reuses that, so there's nothing new to learn.

Driver names, faces, plates and the map labels are blurred.

Solution

Your car first. Help one tap away.

Assumptions: a call center can triage every SOS, and technicians can go out right away. Both are questions for operations.

Solution · Screen 1

The home screen reacts to the car.

Same screen, two moments. When the battery is due, the card turns amber and the offers switch to batteries that fit.

The countdown needs data from a screen I didn't design: the odometer and the last battery date. I kept Tapsi Garage's 3D icons because they already fit Snapp, and only added a green accent.

Solution · Screen 1 · Iterations

Seven drafts of one home screen.

My first draft put a lottery banner above the car. That's promotion saturation, and H8 says cut it. Each draft removed something.

Solution · Screen 1 · A/B test

Four tab bars. A test should pick one.

SOS has to be found in a second without shouting over the rest of the app. I picked C from my benchmarks, but an A/B test should decide, not me.

What I'd measure: time from opening the app to tapping SOS, and taps on SOS by mistake.

Solution · Screen 2

Only batteries that fit.

The car is picked before the list loads. Each card says in plain words if it fits, and whether it's installed at home or at a shop.

Two versions: a bigger banner for marketing and partner deals, or more batteries above the fold. The banner also points planned drivers to the fast on-site service.

Fixes C1 · C2 · C3

Solution · Screen 3

SOS: a call, not a checkout.

"Won't start" can be the battery or the alternator, and a stressed driver can't tell. So an expert calls first, and a technician only goes out with the right part.

  1. Tap SOSThe moment before the sheet opens
  2. What happened?Won't start, or "I don't know"
  3. Where are you?GPS, or say it on the phone
  4. Finding an expertCost agreed on the call, paid after
  5. Call or cancelAlways one tap away
  6. On the wayArrival time and who's coming
  7. DetailsLocation, safety, cancel

No choices, no price to pick, no national ID, no partner web page. I think SOS deserves its own business line at Tapsi Garage; this flow is my proposal for it.

Bonus · Next steps

With more time, I'd…

  1. Test with both users. Five who've been stranded, five planning a swap.
  2. Run the tab bar A/B test on time to find SOS.
  3. Check Android. Native back gesture, Material patterns, smaller screens.
  4. Prototype the bottom sheets and every SOS state.
  5. Pitch SOS as its own line to Tapsi Garage, with operations: call center, dispatch time, bringing the partner page in-app.
  6. Set real baselines for the three key results.
